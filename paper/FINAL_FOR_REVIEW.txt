ABSTRACT
================================================================================

NVIDIA reports that MXFP4 needs 36% more training tokens than NVFP4 to reach the
same pretraining loss on its own 8B model, but the two formats differ in two ways
at once, block size and scale format, so that result cannot say which choice is
responsible. This work separates them. Backward error is measured on a quantized
GEMM under exact accumulation, isolating input quantization from accumulator
rounding, with classical rounding-error analysis adapted to block-scaled formats.
Block size and scale format are crossed in a two-by-two design and swept over
operand tail weight and contraction length. The scale format moves measured error
more than the block size does at every canonical cell, and the ranking survives
stochastic rounding and a random Hadamard transform; the margin narrows sharply
at the heaviest tail without crossing. The originally planned comparison,
locating the tail index at which the derived bound first fails, proved
uninformative: the bound is broken across the entire tested range, which
motivated the magnitude-based analysis reported here. Against GPT-2 activations
the predictions transfer closely at two of three layers, with the third traced to
channel-concentrated outliers.


================================================================================
1  INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

NVIDIA reports that MXFP4 matches NVFP4's pretraining loss only when trained on
36% more tokens -- 1.36T against 1T -- on its own 8B hybrid Mamba-Transformer
model [NVIDIA, 2025]: the vendor's own figure, not independently established
here, and the observation that motivates this work.

What the figure cannot say is which design choice earns the difference. The two
formats store identical E2M1 elements and differ in their scaling alone, but they
differ in two ways at once: NVFP4 scales blocks of 16 elements with an E4M3 scale
factor, MXFP4 blocks of 32 with a power-of-two E8M0 factor. Any gap measured
between the two presets is therefore attributable to the block size, to the scale
format, or to an interaction between them, with no way to tell which from the
comparison itself.

This work separates them. Block size and scale format are crossed in a 2x2
factorial design, so either can be held fixed while the other varies. Accuracy is
measured as the backward error of a quantized GEMM, using classical
rounding-error analysis adapted to block-scaled formats (Section 3). The design
is swept over operand tail weight and contraction length, so the comparison rests
on a grid of configurations rather than on a single measurement.

The scale format, not the block size, is found to be the dominant factor.

3.1  Setup: Backward Error with Exact Accumulation

The measurement device is a quantized GEMM. For matrices A (M x n) and B (n x N),
both are quantized using a block-scaled low-precision format along the contraction
dimension n, and the resulting float64 reconstructions – the dequantized
The values, not the integer representations, are multiplied. The accuracy is
assessed elementwise using the backward error [Rasquinha and Tabak, 2023]

(Eq. 1)   BE_ij  =  | (AB)_ij - C-hat_ij |  /  ( |A|·|B| )_ij

In this case, C-hat stands for the quantized product, and the absolute value operation is applied element by element.
denominator is calculated using the product of absolute values, not |AB|; the two
differ when the exact product involves cancellation.

The main approach accumulates the reconstructions exactly, in float64 – a
was a deliberate design choice. A low-precision GEMM has two kinds of error: errors in the operands
snapped onto a coarse grid, and partial sums rounded in a narrow accumulator;
combined, they obscure which one drives the measurement. Exact accumulation
removes the second, so the residual C-hat - AB in (Eq. 1) is input-quantization
error alone. A bfloat16 accumulator is a secondary option, excluded from this
Grid's analysis and this was not included in the primary results.

Operands have i.i.d. t-Student(ν) entries – smaller ν meaning heavier tails,
ν -> ∞ the Gaussian case – each tensor normalized to unit median absolute
There is a deviation, since the standard deviation is not defined when ν is less than or equal to 2.

---

3.2  Reasons the Classical Bound Does Not Apply

The classical worst-case backward-error bound for a length-n inner product is
gamma_n = n·u / (1 - n·u) under unit roundoff u [Higham, 2002]. Two probabilistic
results relax that leading factor, each a high-probability upper bound rather than
a statement about typical growth. Higham and Mary replace gamma_n by a relaxed
constant of order sqrt(n log n)·u, holding with a probability bounded below, for
rounding errors modelled as mean-independent random variables [Higham and Mary,
2019]; Connolly, Higham and Mary prove a sqrt(n)·u bound with no log factor,
unconditionally, for stochastic rounding specifically [Connolly et al., 2021].
The two cover different rounding regimes rather than refining one another.

The n-dependence of both families stems specifically from repeated rounding
during accumulation: a single rounding occurs during every summation step,
totaling n, either compounding in the worst case (gamma_n) or behaving as a
random walk (sqrt(n)·u). Exact accumulation removes that mechanism, so neither
bound’s n-dependence applies here. Additionally, a block-scaled format does not
provide a fixed u to replace: the classical unit roundoff is half an ulp based on
a grid defined by the encoding, while here, the grid that an element occupies is
determined by the largest magnitude in its own block, resulting in the achieved
relative error being a distribution rather than an attribute of the format.

---

3.3  A Measured Unit Roundoff, and the Constructed Bound

The quantity that substitutes for u is measured directly. For a specific element
format, block size, scale format, and tail index ν, define

(Eq. 2)   u_eff(format, block, ν)  =  median over i of  ( |x_i - x-hat_i| / |x_i| )

This is the median per-element relative quantization error for that ν and
configuration; its 99th percentile is recorded alongside. Estimation uses a
distinct calibration draw from an independent stream, so the denominator in the
ratio tested below is never taken from the sample it is evaluated against.

Relative error is ill-defined as x_i -> 0, and “near zero” is meaningful only
relative to an element’s own effective scale s_eff (block scale times global
scale, where one is used), since block scales span many orders of magnitude
across a heavy-tailed tensor. Elements with |x_i| <= 0.25·s_eff are therefore
excluded. The threshold is not tuned: E2M1’s smallest nonzero magnitude is
0.5·s_eff, so under round-to-nearest any element at or below 0.25·s_eff quantizes
to exactly zero with relative error 1 – the format’s dynamic-range floor, not
its correctness. That the cut is necessary was checked, not assumed: with it
disabled, all 32 measured configurations return p99 = 1.00000 exactly. Its price
is a large, configuration-dependent exclusion – surviving fractions range from
0.360 (MXFP4 at ν = 1) to 0.932 (NVFP4 at the Gaussian end) – tabulated wherever
u_eff is reported.

With exact accumulation, the error at the output entry (i,j) consists of a sum of
n one-time input-quantization errors with essentially random signs, such that
partial cancellation results in its growth resembling sqrt(n) instead of n, while
the denominator in Equation (1), which is the sum over k of |A_ik| times |B_kj|, involves n strictly positive terms.
The terms increase and grow approximately linearly in the case of well-behaved inputs. Together, these
result in BE ~ sqrt(n)·u_eff / n, leading to the tested bound

(Eq. 3)   beta(n, format, block, ν)  =  c · u_eff(format, block, ν) / sqrt(n)

which decays in n rather than grows. This is an order-of-magnitude argument of
the central-limit/law-of-large-numbers kind, not a proven tight inequality, and
is not offered as a theoretical contribution: both premises require moments the
heaviest tails swept here lack, and at ν = 1 (Cauchy) the reasoning is expected
to break down – a limit of its assumptions, not a defect in the form of (Eq. 3).

The constant c is predetermined at c = 1 for all supporting analyses, in
accordance with the anti-circularity principle established in the preregistration
of this study: a bound whose constant is fitted to the data it evaluates cannot
be refuted by that same data. Adopting a unit leading constant is consistent with
the tradition of probabilistic rounding analyses, which replace n·u with
sqrt(n)·u in a deterministic factor without introducing a fitted constant
[Connolly et al., 2021]. Given that the calculation is order-of-magnitude rather
than tight, a poorly calibrated c = 1 serves as a reportable finding instead of
justification for recalibration; any estimation of c-hat derived from data is still descriptive and exploratory, not reintegrated into the confirmatory
ratio.

The bound is deemed broken at (format, ν, n) when the lower limit of the 95%
bootstrap confidence interval of the median of BE/beta(n) exceeds 1.0: indicating
when the error reduces at a rate that is notably slower than anticipated
by the cancellation rate, rather than simply exceeding a growing worst-case
The fact that u_eff is a function of ν means that both sides of this ratio change as ν varies.
testing whether the scaling law endures despite heavy tails, rather than
evaluating whether heavy tails increase absolute error.

---

3.4  Factorial design

Because MXFP4 and NVFP4 differ in block size and scale format at once, the design
crosses the two factors – block size in {16, 32}, scale format in {E8M0, E4M3}
– so either can be held fixed while the other varies. This 2x2 setup is the main
causal comparison. The element format is E2M1 everywhere, since both real formats
use it, so scaling is the only thing that changes. The per-tensor global scale
comes from the scale format rather than being varied separately. E8M0 covers 255
binades and can reach any block’s magnitude on its own, while E4M3 covers about
19 and overflows to NaN when one is not provided. The rounding performed by this project for the MXFP4 block scale
exponent up rather than down (OCP/microxcaling’s default), matching NVIDIA’s own
stated training practice [NVIDIA, 2025] and verified bit-exact against
microxcaling in this project’s own testing.

Two of the four combinations are real formats. Block 32 with an E8M0 scale is
MXFP4, and block 16 with an E4M3 scale is NVFP4. The other two, block 16 with
E8M0 and block 32 with E4M3, are experimental controls. They do not correspond
to any hardware, specification, or vendor format, and exist only as measurement
tools to make the comparison between the two real formats clear. This paper does
not propose, endorse, or benchmark them as formats, and the results tables keep
this distinction clear.

Rounding mode (either round-to-nearest-even or stochastic) and a random Hadamard
transform (on or off) are secondary factors and are not part of the main causal
comparison. The transform is applied to each block along the contraction
dimension with the same random signs on both operands. This way, it cancels out
inside every inner product and does not need inversion.

These four factors are combined with the tail index, which runs over
ν in {1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 15, 30} together with the Gaussian limit, and with the
contraction dimension n in {16, 64, 256, 1024, 4096}, creating a grid of
8 x 5 x 2^4 = 640 cells, all using exact accumulation. The primary contraction
dimension is n = 4096. It was not fixed in advance: it was declared post hoc in a
dated preregistration amendment, filed before any confirmatory ν* analysis was
run but after the sweep itself and its n-scaling slope fits were already
complete, on the stated grounds that 4096 is closest to real GEMM shapes in
language-model workloads and is the n at which the sqrt(n)-decay derivation’s

asymptotic assumptions are most likely to be valid. The remaining four values are used as
stability checks, reported as a separate sensitivity table and never substituted
for it. Trial counts are adaptive. There are 5000 trials per cell at
ν in {1, 2, 3}, where quantile estimates are noisiest and the bound is most
likely to break, and 1000 trials per cell for the lighter tails, making a total
of 1.6 million trials.

---

3.5  Robust statistical protocol

Each trial provides a  summary of its own M x N backward-error matrix, and the
trial is the resampling unit throughout, never the individual matrix element.
Elements within a block share a scale factor, are not exchangeable, and would
give anticonservative intervals that inflate the measured break rate. Confidence
intervals use the percentile bootstrap, with B = 10,000 resamples and an
explicitly seeded generator, and are reported as computed rather than forced to
be symmetric.

The median and 90th percentile carry all confirmatory statistical weight. The
99th percentile is reported for every cell but is only indicative, at any trial
count used here. The reason is a measured coverage gap, not a generic caveat
about percentile bootstraps. A cell’s p99 is determined by roughly its top 1% of
values (10 out of 1000 trials, 50 out of 5000), and a percentile bootstrap
resamples with replacement from the observed sample, so it can reweight those
values but can never produce a value more extreme than the largest already drawn.
A coverage simulation measures this: 1000 simulated experiments per cell,
t-Student(ν = 2), both trial budgets, nominal 95%. Median coverage is 95.5% at
1000 trials and 94.8% at 5000, which is close to nominal at both. The p99
coverage is 92.7% and 94.0%, which is below the median’s at both budgets and
about 3.5 standard errors less than nominal at 1000 trials. The gap is real and in
the direction the mechanism forecasts, but modest, not a collapse. More trials
help p99 measurably without closing it – hence the indicative-only status at
every trial count.

No configuration’s interval contains -0.5. All 128 lie entirely above it: the
error decays with n, but more slowly than the cancellation regime predicts. None
decays faster, and none has a positive slope, so error grows with n nowhere in
the grid. That unanimity alone overstates the finding, however. It rests on a
significance test, and at 1000-5000 trials per cell the intervals exclude -0.5
even when the point estimate misses it by a few thousandths. Splitting by
magnitude instead, at |slope - (-0.5)| > 0.02, separates the two readings:
63 of the 128 configurations deviate materially from -0.5, and 65 do not.

The split is ordered by tail weight, and the deviation shrinks smoothly and
monotonically as the tail lightens. At ν in {1, 2, 3} every one of the 16
sub-configurations is material, with gaps from 0.041 to 0.490; the extreme is
ν = 1, where the fitted slopes run from -0.010 to -0.139, one to two orders of
magnitude shallower than predicted rather than borderline. At ν in {5, 8} the
sub-configurations straddle the threshold, some above and some below, consistent
with a smooth transition rather than a sharp cutoff. At ν in {15, 30, Gaussian}
none is material at any configuration, the gap staying between 0.004 and 0.020.

The split follows the derivation itself’s own premises. Section 3.3 obtained the -0.5
exponent from a mean-zero numerator behaving like a random walk and a denominator
obeying a law of large numbers – both resting based on moments the heaviest tails here
lack; at ν = 1 the t-Student is Cauchy, with neither finite mean nor variance.
The exponent is recovered to within two hundredths exactly where those moments
exist, and departs from it exactly where they do not (Figure 1).

4.2  Scale Format, Not Block Size, Is the Larger Lever

The 2x2 design is read directly off the measured backward error, with no
theoretical bound involved at any point: no beta(n), no c, no ratio to 1. This partition is exploratory and post-hoc, adopted under a dated preregistration
amendment (Sec. 8.4) once the preregistered confirmatory comparison – P1-P3 of
Sec. 4, all framed in terms of ν* – was left undefined by the finding of Section
4.3. For each combination of tail index, contraction dimension, rounding mode and
Hadamard setting, three statistics are formed in log space,

effect_block  =  median(log BE | block=16)   -  median(log BE | block=32)
effect_scale  =  median(log BE | scale=E4M3) -  median(log BE | scale=E8M0)
interaction   =  [ (16,E4M3) - (32,E4M3) ]   -  [ (16,E8M0) - (32,E8M0) ]

each marginal effect aggregating over the other factor, with a 95%
percentile-bootstrap interval from 2000 replicates resampling all four underlying
cells in one shared pass. Neither the tail index nor n is collapsed.

Scale format moves the measured error more than block size does at every one of
the 40 canonical cells (8 ν x 5 n, round-to-nearest, no Hadamard transform),
and the ranking survives the secondary factors: scale format leads in 34 of 40
cells with the Hadamard transform on, 38 of 40 during stochastic rounding, and 35
of 40 with both, never reversing to an overall block-size majority in any of the
four combinations. At n = 4096, canonically, effect_scale ranges from -0.162 at
the Gaussian end to -0.389 at ν = 2, against -0.042 to -0.246 for effect_block: a
pull two to four times larger across the grid (Figure 2). Both effects are
negative at every n >= 64, so block = 16 and E4M3 – NVFP4’s two choices – each
reduce measured error relative to their alternatives, and the larger of the two
reductions is the scale format.

The ranking is identical at all 5 values of n, at every ν, with no crossing
anywhere in the grid, so the practical answer here – unlike the threshold
analysis of Section 4.3 – does not depend on which n had been designated
primary. The n = 16 column is the apparent exception, and it is not measuring
block size at all: zero-padding leaves max|x| unchanged, so a nominal block size
of 32 spans the same 16 elements, with the same scale, as block size 16 does. The
effect first becomes measurable at n = 64, where the two arms are genuinely
different quantizers, and is essentially flat through n = 4096 (at ν = 1: -0.286,
-0.270, -0.257, -0.246).

The margin between the two factors is not uniform, and it is thinnest at the
heaviest tail. Written as |effect_scale| - |effect_block|, it is 0.016 to 0.043
at ν = 1 for n >= 64, against 0.124 to 0.135 at ν = 15 – a four- to eightfold
narrowing – and the narrowing is confined to ν = 1, since at ν = 2 the margin is
0.16 to 0.19, the widest of the three tail indices examined. The margin narrows
sharply at ν = 1 but does not cross. Point estimates never flip at any of the
40 canonical cells, and at n = 4096 the intervals do not overlap:
|effect_block| in [0.2395, 0.2510] against |effect_scale| in [0.2719, 0.2831].

This only partly fits the per-element picture. On u_eff (the 2x2 applied to
elementwise rather than end-to-end error), the block-size effect leads at ν = 1
and the two cross by ν = 2; in backward error the direction replicates but the
crossing does not, the scale-format lead narrowing toward ν = 1 without
reversing.

The two factors are not purely additive: the interaction term is significant at
essentially every cell with n >= 64, from -0.095 at ν = 1 to -0.028 at the
Gaussian end at n = 4096. It is not folded into the dominance comparison above,
which uses the pooled marginal effects.

4.3  Why the Analysis Is Framed by Magnitude and Not by a Threshold

That decomposition replaced a preregistered plan: locate ν*, the tail index at
which beta(n) first fails by the criterion of Section 3.3, for each of the 16
families, and compare the two block sizes by where their ν* falls.

The bound is broken at every one of the 8 tested tail indices, in every one of
the 16 families, at n_primary = 4096, and 0 of the 16 is non-monotone, so ν* is not simply hard to locate but undefined everywhere on this grid. For the two
named presets, canonically, the median ratio at ν = 1 is 31.92 [31.85, 31.97] for
MXFP4 and 20.91 [20.85, 20.97] for NVFP4, against 1.588 [1.585, 1.590] and 1.515
[1.513, 1.517] at the Gaussian limit. Even in the near-Gaussian regime, which the

calculation’s assumptions fit best, the interval sits entirely above 1: c = 1
under-predicts the measured error by roughly 50 to 60% there, and by a factor of
20 to 32 at ν = 1 (Figure 3).

This is not an artifact of the primary contraction dimension: the same pattern
holds at all 5 values of n, 16 of 16 families censored above and 0 below at each.
For MXFP4 at ν = 1 the ratio is already 3.55 at n = 16 and rises through 7.18,
12.10 and 19.62 to 31.92 at n = 4096, growing in line with the
shallower-than-(-0.5) exponent of Section 4.1 rather than closing.

The preregistration anticipates this outcome: its all-cells-censored-above
contingency states the tail range was mis-specified, the hypothesis untestable on
such a grid, and the result to be reported as a grid mis-specification, not a
confirmation. It is equally not a null – the rank comparison returns equal
fragility for the two block sizes only because every rank is pinned at the
ceiling, the signature of a saturated measurement. The two confirmatory results
therefore separate cleanly: the functional form of (Eq. 3) is well supported at
ν >= 15 (Section 4.1), while its leading constant, fixed at c = 1 by the
anti-circularity constraint of Section 3.3, is not calibrated anywhere in the
tested range.

With no ν* to locate, block size and scale format could not be compared through
the bound at all – both sides are censored past the edge of what was measured.
This is why Section 4.2 addresses the same practical question directly from
measured magnitudes, rather than through the threshold criterion above.

5  VALIDATION WITH REAL ACTIVATIONS
================================================================================

The t-Student model underlying every result above was checked against the
activations a real network produces. GPT-2 small was run over 300 non-empty lines
of the WikiText-2 raw test split, giving 34,390 valid tokens, and activations
were captured at three of its twelve transformer blocks -- h0, h6 and h11, the
first, middle and last -- all at the attention input projection (attn.c_attn), so
that depth is the only thing varying across the three. Padded positions are
removed before any statistic or GEMM operand sees them. Tail weight is summarized
by excess kurtosis in Fisher's convention, in which a Gaussian is 0 and not 3,
computed over the full flattened tensor as a single distribution.

Each layer's excess kurtosis k is mapped to an equivalent tail index through
ν = 4 + 6/k, the closed-form inverse of a t-Student's k = 6/(ν - 4), which is
defined only for ν > 4. The out-of-domain case is k <= 0, which no t-Student
attains and which the code reports as N/A with a stated reason rather than
extrapolating; it did not arise at any layer, though h6's ν = 4.06 sits within
0.06 of the boundary. The quantized-GEMM pipeline of Section 3.1 is then run
twice per layer and preset: once on the real activations, once on a synthetic
t-Student(ν) draw of identical shape, rescaled so its median absolute deviation
equals the real tensor's. The layer's own weight matrix serves as operand B in
both arms, so the two runs differ only in operand A's tail shape.

Four of the six layer-preset cells land within 10% of the t-Student prediction,
and none falls outside 50%. At h0 (excess kurtosis 1.02, ν = 9.88) and h11 (7.03,
ν = 4.85) the observed median(BE) equals the predicted value to within 2-4%
under both presets: observed/predicted ratios of 0.997 and 0.987 at h0, 1.041 and
0.994 at h11. Layer h6 misses. Its excess kurtosis is 101.9, two orders of
magnitude beyond the other two layers, its equivalent ν is 4.06, and its observed
error runs 38% (MXFP4) and 23% (NVFP4) *higher* than predicted. The direction is
the informative part: the real activations produce *more* backward error than a
MAD- and kurtosis-matched synthetic draw, so a single matched scalar
under-describes how concentrated h6's outlier mass is.

A targeted diagnostic on h6's own tensor, with no GEMM rerun, locates that
concentration. The twenty largest activation magnitudes in the entire
34,390 x 768 tensor fall in exactly one channel, index 64, across twenty distinct
tokens. Within the top-1% pool the same channel accounts for 13.0% of the mass
and appears for all 34,390 tokens, the five most frequent channels for 58.2%, and
the top ten for 75.3%; per-channel maxima drop sharply after a handful (8.06,
7.08, 6.55, 5.31, then 2.76 for the next channel down). This is the
massive-activations pattern documented in transformer hidden states
[Sun et al., 2024], and it violates an assumption the synthetic model makes
implicitly: that large values are randomly located and redrawn every sample,
where h6's are fixed to the same few channels on essentially every token. The
measured value also falls in a gap this study's own ν-grid never samples:
ν in {1, 2, 3} have undefined population excess kurtosis, outside the closed
form's ν > 4 domain, while ν >= 5 tops out at 6 for the heaviest finite-kurtosis
grid point -- so 101.9 is finite but far past any value the grid covers. Whether
that framework mechanistically drives the 23-38% gap, rather than merely
associated with it, is not established here -- no ablation separating channel
structure from the kurtosis match was run, and none is claimed.

This check is exploratory and bears on none of the confirmatory comparisons. It
tests the study's modeling choice, not any hypothesis about the formats, and
finds it sound across most of a real network -- failing only under extreme,
channel-concentrated kurtosis, where a single kurtosis-matched ν is insufficient.


================================================================================
6  RELATED WORK AND LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

This work builds directly on the backward-error methodology of Rasquinha and
Tabak [2023], extending it to block-scaled formats and heavy-tailed operands. Two
recent works approach FP4 microscaling from other directions. Fasoli et al.
[2026] also measures block-size effects on model correctness, but attributes
degradation to *narrow* tensor distributions -- the opposite regime from the
heavy tails swept here. Egiazarian et al. [2026] analyses MXFP4 and NVFP4 error
under a Laplace operand model scored by per-element MSE, differing from this work
in both metric and operand model.

Six scope limits qualify these results. (i) The three per-tensor reference
formats -- FP8-E4M3, FP8-E5M2 and INT8 -- are in the frozen grid but never
executed, leaving MXFP4 and NVFP4 without a classical per-tensor baseline.
(ii) The random Hadamard transform is applied per block: a global, whole-row
transform would spread energy over sqrt(n), tying its effect to the same n whose
scaling law is under study, so global RHT is not evaluated here. (iii)
Quantization is CPU emulation in float64, not real quantized hardware. (iv) Only
E2M1 elements are studied. (v) Elements sharing a block share a scale, so their
errors are correlated rather than independent -- the leading candidate behind the
u_eff residual, consistent with a survival-fraction gap whose rank order against
the u_eff block-size excess is perfect across all eight ν (Spearman 1.0), but not
isolated by a dedicated ablation. (vi) The fixed c = 1 is fixed a priori for
anti-circularity, so what is characterized is the bound's functional *form*, not
a calibrated absolute value.


================================================================================
7  CONCLUSION
================================================================================

Two design choices separate NVFP4 from MXFP4, and they do not contribute equally.
Across the full grid of tail weights and contraction lengths, the scale format
moves the measured backward error more than the block size does, at every
canonical cell, and the ranking survives both secondary factors -- stochastic
rounding and the random Hadamard transform -- without reversing anywhere. The
margin narrows at the heaviest tail without crossing. This is a post-hoc
decomposition of measured magnitudes, not a preregistered test, and it involves
no theoretical bound at any point.

That last property is what the threshold analysis leaves behind. The constructed
bound breaks across the whole tested range, so the tail index at which it first
fails is undefined everywhere on this grid, and could not carry the planned
comparison; that null motivated the pivot to comparing magnitudes directly, and
does not bear on the decomposition, which never uses the bound. Two steps would
sharpen the picture: executing the specified but unrun per-tensor reference
cells, which would place both formats against a classical baseline, and an
ablation separating the intra-block correlation mechanism from other contributors
to the u_eff residual.
