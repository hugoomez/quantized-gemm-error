===============================================================================
1. INTRODUCTION
===============================================================================


[1-P1]
NVIDIA reports that MXFP4 matches NVFP4's pretraining loss only when trained
on 36% more tokens -- 1.36T against 1T -- on its own 8B hybrid
Mamba-Transformer model {{cita: nvidia2026nvfp4}}: the vendor's own figure,
not independently established here, and the observation that motivates this
work.

[1-P2]
What the figure cannot say is which design choice earns the difference. The
two formats store identical E2M1 elements and differ in their scaling alone,
but they differ in two ways at once: NVFP4 scales blocks of 16 elements with
an E4M3 scale factor, MXFP4 blocks of 32 with a power-of-two E8M0 factor. Any
gap measured between the two presets is therefore attributable to the block
size, to the scale format, or to an interaction between them, with no way to
tell which from the comparison itself.

[1-P3]
This work separates them. Block size and scale format are crossed in a 2x2
factorial design, so either can be held fixed while the other varies; the two
deployed formats sit at diagonal corners of the resulting grid, and the
off-diagonal cells correspond to no hardware and no specification, existing
only as measurement instruments. Accuracy is measured as the backward error
of a quantized GEMM, using the machinery of classical rounding-error analysis
adapted to formats whose grid is set by the block an element falls in rather
than by the encoding alone -- an adaptation Section 3 develops. The design is
swept over operand tail weight and contraction length, so the comparison rests
on a grid of configurations rather than on a single measurement.

[1-P4]
The scale format, not the block size, is found to be the dominant factor.
