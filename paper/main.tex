\begin{abstract}
  NVIDIA reports that MXFP4 needs 36\% more training tokens than NVFP4 to reach
  the same pretraining loss on its own 8B model, but the two formats differ in
  two ways at once, block size and scale format, so that result cannot say which
  choice is responsible. This work separates them. Backward error of a quantized
  GEMM is measured under exact accumulation, isolating input quantization from
  accumulator rounding, within a framework in the tradition of classical
  rounding-error analysis but adapted where that analysis no longer applies.
  Block size and
  scale format are crossed in a two-by-two design and swept over operand tail
  weight and contraction length. The scale format moves measured error more than
  the block size does at every canonical cell, and the ranking survives
  stochastic rounding and a random Hadamard transform; the margin narrows sharply
  at the heaviest tail without crossing. The originally planned comparison,
  locating the tail index at which the derived bound first fails, proved
  uninformative: the bound is broken across the entire tested range, which
  motivated the magnitude-based analysis reported here. Against GPT-2 activations
  the predictions transfer closely at two of three layers, with the third showing a
  mismatch consistent with channel-concentrated outliers, not established as
  causal here.
\end{abstract}
