\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

NVIDIA reports that MXFP4 matches NVFP4's pretraining loss only when trained on
36\% more tokens --- 1.36T against 1T --- on its own 8B hybrid Mamba-Transformer
model \citep{nvidia2026nvfp4}: the vendor's own figure, not independently
established here, and the observation that motivates this work.

What the figure cannot say is which design choice earns the difference. The two
formats differ in their scaling in two ways at once: NVFP4 scales blocks of 16
elements with an E4M3 factor, MXFP4 blocks of 32 with a power-of-two E8M0 factor.
Any gap between the two presets is therefore attributable to block size, scale
format, or their interaction, with no way to tell which from the comparison
itself.

This work separates them. Block size and scale format are crossed in a
$2\times2$ factorial design, so either can be held fixed while the other varies.
Accuracy is measured as the backward error of a quantized GEMM, in the tradition
of classical rounding-error analysis but adapted where that analysis does not
apply under exact accumulation (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
The design is swept over operand tail weight and contraction length, so the
comparison rests on a grid, not a single measurement.

The scale format, not the block size, is the dominant factor for this
backward-error proxy.
