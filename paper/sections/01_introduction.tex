\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

NVIDIA reports that MXFP4 matches NVFP4's pretraining loss only when trained on
36\% more tokens --- 1.36T against 1T --- on its own 8B hybrid Mamba-Transformer
model \citep{nvidia2026nvfp4}: the vendor's own figure, not independently
established here, and the observation that motivates this work.

What the figure cannot say is which design choice earns the difference. The two
formats store identical E2M1 elements and differ in their scaling alone, but they
differ in two ways at once: NVFP4 scales blocks of 16 elements with an E4M3 scale
factor, MXFP4 blocks of 32 with a power-of-two E8M0 factor. Any gap measured
between the two presets is therefore attributable to the block size, to the scale
format, or to an interaction between them, with no way to tell which from the
comparison itself.

This work separates them. Block size and scale format are crossed in a
$2\times2$ factorial design, so either can be held fixed while the other varies.
Accuracy is measured as the backward error of a quantized GEMM, using classical
rounding-error analysis adapted to block-scaled formats (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
The design is swept over operand tail weight and contraction length, so the
comparison rests on a grid of configurations rather than on a single measurement.

The scale format, not the block size, is found to be the dominant factor.
