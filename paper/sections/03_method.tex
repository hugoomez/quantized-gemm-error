\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup: Backward Error with Exact Accumulation}
\label{sec:method:setup}

The measurement device is a quantized GEMM. For matrices $A$ ($M \times n$) and
$B$ ($n \times N$), both are quantized using a block-scaled low-precision format
along the contraction dimension $n$, and the resulting float64 reconstructions
--- the dequantized values, not the integer representations --- are multiplied.
The accuracy is assessed elementwise using the backward error
\citep{rasquinha2023metric}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{BE}_{ij} \;=\; \frac{\bigl|(AB)_{ij} - \hat{C}_{ij}\bigr|}
                              {\bigl(|A|\,|B|\bigr)_{ij}}
  \label{eq:be}
\end{equation}
Here $\hat{C}$ is the quantized product and $|\cdot|$ acts elementwise. The
denominator uses the product of absolute values, not $|AB|$; the two differ when
the exact product involves cancellation.

The main route accumulates the reconstructions exactly, in float64. A
low-precision GEMM has two error sources --- operands snapped onto a coarse grid,
and partial sums rounded in a narrow accumulator --- which combined obscure which
one drives the measurement. Exact accumulation removes the second, so the
residual $\hat{C} - AB$ in (\ref{eq:be}) is input-quantization error alone. A
bfloat16 accumulator is a secondary option, not used in the primary results.

Operands have i.i.d.\ t-Student($\nu$) entries --- smaller $\nu$ meaning heavier
tails, $\nu \to \infty$ the Gaussian case --- each tensor normalized to unit
median absolute deviation, since the standard deviation is not defined
when $\nu$ is less than or equal to 2.

\subsection{Reasons the Classical Bound Does Not Apply}
\label{sec:method:classical}

The classical worst-case backward-error bound for a length-$n$ inner product is
$\gamma_n = n\,u/(1-n\,u)$ under unit roundoff $u$ \citep{higham2002accuracy}.
Two probabilistic results relax that leading factor, each a high-probability
upper bound rather than a statement about typical growth. Higham and Mary give a
high-probability bound of order $\sqrt{n \log n}\,u$ for rounding errors modelled
as mean-independent random variables \citep{higham2019new}; Connolly, Higham and
Mary prove an unconditional $\sqrt{n}\,u$ bound (no log factor) specific to
stochastic rounding \citep{connolly2021stochastic}. The two cover different
rounding regimes rather than refining one another.

The $n$-dependence of both families stems specifically from repeated rounding
during accumulation: a single rounding occurs during every summation step,
totaling $n$, either compounding in the worst case ($\gamma_n$) or behaving as a
random walk ($\sqrt{n}\,u$). Exact accumulation removes that mechanism, so
neither bound's $n$-dependence applies here. A block-scaled format also provides
no fixed $u$ to replace: classical unit roundoff is half an ulp on a grid fixed
by the encoding, whereas here an element's grid is set by the largest magnitude
in its block, so the achieved relative error is a distribution, not a property of
the format.

\subsection{A Measured Unit Roundoff, and the Constructed Bound}
\label{sec:method:bound}

The quantity that substitutes for $u$ is measured directly. For a specific
element format, block size, scale format, and tail index $\nu$, define
\begin{equation}
  u_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathrm{format}, \mathrm{block}, \nu)
  \;=\; \mathrm{median}_{\,i}\;
        \frac{\bigl|x_i - \hat{x}_i\bigr|}{\bigl|x_i\bigr|}
  \label{eq:ueff}
\end{equation}
\looseness=-1 This is the median per-element relative quantization error for that
$\nu$ and configuration; its 99th percentile is recorded alongside. Estimation
uses a distinct calibration draw from an independent stream, so the denominator in
the ratio tested below is never taken from the sample it is evaluated against.

Relative error is ill-defined as $x_i \to 0$, and ``near zero'' is meaningful only
relative to an element's own effective scale $s_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (block scale times
global scale, where one is used), since block scales span many orders of magnitude
across a heavy-tailed tensor. Elements with $|x_i| \le 0.25\,s_{\mathrm{eff}}$ are
therefore excluded. The threshold is not tuned: E2M1's smallest nonzero magnitude
is $0.5\,s_{\mathrm{eff}}$, so under round-to-nearest any element at or below
$0.25\,s_{\mathrm{eff}}$ quantizes to exactly zero with relative error $1$ --- the
format's dynamic-range floor, not its precision. That the cut is necessary was
checked, not assumed: with it disabled, all $32$ measured configurations return
$p_{99} = 1.00000$ exactly. Its price is a large, configuration-dependent
exclusion --- surviving fractions range from $0.360$ (MXFP4 at $\nu = 1$) to
$0.932$ (NVFP4 at the Gaussian end) --- tabulated wherever $u_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is
reported.

With exact accumulation, the error at the output entry $(i,j)$ consists of a sum
of $n$ one-time input-quantization errors with essentially random signs, such
that partial cancellation results in its growth resembling $\sqrt{n}$ instead of
$n$, while the denominator in Equation~(\ref{eq:be}), which is
$\sum_k |A_{ik}|\,|B_{kj}|$, involves $n$ strictly positive terms. The terms
increase and grow approximately linearly in the case of well-behaved inputs.
Together, these result in $\mathrm{BE} \sim \sqrt{n}\,u_{\mathrm{eff}}/n$, leading
to the tested bound
\begin{equation}
  \beta(n, \mathrm{format}, \mathrm{block}, \nu)
  \;=\; c \cdot
  \frac{u_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathrm{format}, \mathrm{block}, \nu)}{\sqrt{n}}
  \label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
which decays in $n$ rather than grows. This is an order-of-magnitude argument of
the central-limit/law-of-large-numbers kind, not a proven tight inequality, and
is not offered as a theoretical contribution: both premises require moments the
heaviest tails swept here lack, and at $\nu = 1$ (Cauchy) the reasoning is
expected to break down --- a limit of its assumptions, not a defect in the form
of (\ref{eq:bound}).

The constant $c$ is predetermined at $c = 1$ for all supporting analyses, in
accordance with the anti-circularity principle established in the preregistration
of this study: a bound whose constant is fitted to the data it evaluates cannot
be refuted by that same data. Given that the calculation is order-of-magnitude
rather than tight, a poorly calibrated $c = 1$ serves as a reportable finding
instead of justification for recalibration; any estimation of $\hat{c}$ derived
from data is still descriptive and exploratory, not reintegrated into the
confirmatory ratio. The preregistration document, including all dated amendments,
is provided as supplementary material.

\looseness=-1 The bound is deemed broken at $(\mathrm{format}, \nu, n)$ when the
lower limit of the 95\% bootstrap CI of the median $\mathrm{BE}/\beta(n)$ exceeds
$1.0$ --- i.e.\ the error decays notably slower than the cancellation rate
predicts, not merely exceeds a growing worst-case limit. Since $u_{\mathrm{eff}}$
depends on $\nu$, both sides move with $\nu$: this tests whether the scaling law
endures under heavy tails, not whether heavy tails raise absolute error.

\subsection{Factorial design}
\label{sec:method:design}

The design crosses block size $\{16, 32\}$ with scale format
$\{\mathrm{E8M0}, \mathrm{E4M3}\}$; this $2\times2$ is the main causal comparison.
The element format is E2M1 throughout, as in both real formats, so only the
scaling changes, and the per-tensor global scale follows from the scale format
rather than being varied separately. E8M0 reaches any block's magnitude on its
own; E4M3's $\sim$19 binades overflow to NaN without the per-tensor global scale. This project rounds the MXFP4 block-scale
exponent up rather than down (OCP/microxcaling's default), matching NVIDIA's own
stated training practice \citep{nvidia2026nvfp4} and verified bit-exact against
microxcaling in this project's own testing.

Two of the four combinations are real formats: block 32 / E8M0 is MXFP4,
block 16 / E4M3 is NVFP4. The other two (block 16 / E8M0, block 32 / E4M3) have
no hardware or vendor counterpart; they are measurement tools to isolate each
factor, not formats this paper proposes or benchmarks. The results tables mark
the distinction.

Rounding mode (either round-to-nearest-even or stochastic) and a random Hadamard
transform (on or off) are secondary factors and are not part of the main causal
comparison. The transform is applied to each block along the contraction
dimension with the same random signs on both operands. This way, it cancels out
inside every inner product and does not need inversion.

These four factors are combined with the tail index, which runs over
$\nu \in \{1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 15, 30\}$ together with the Gaussian limit, and with
the contraction dimension $n \in \{16, 64, 256, 1024, 4096\}$, creating a grid of
$8 \times 5 \times 2^4 = 640$ cells, all using exact accumulation. The primary
contraction dimension is $n = 4096$, not fixed in advance: it was declared in a
dated preregistration amendment --- after the sweep and its $n$-scaling slope
fits, before any confirmatory $\nu^\star$ analysis --- on the stated grounds that
4096 is closest to real language-model GEMM shapes and is where the
$\sqrt{n}$-decay derivation's asymptotic assumptions are most likely valid.
The remaining four values are used as stability checks, reported as a separate
sensitivity table and never substituted for it. Trial counts are adaptive. There
are $5000$ trials per cell at $\nu \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, where quantile estimates are
noisiest and the bound is most likely to break, and $1000$ trials per cell for
the lighter tails, making a total of $1.6$ million trials.

\subsection{Robust statistical protocol}
\label{sec:method:stats}

Each trial provides a summary of its own $M \times N$ backward-error matrix, and
the trial is the resampling unit throughout, never the individual matrix element.
Elements within a block share a scale factor, are not exchangeable, and would
give anticonservative intervals that inflate the measured break rate. Confidence
intervals use the percentile bootstrap, with $B = 10{,}000$ resamples and an
explicitly seeded generator, and are reported as computed rather than forced to
be symmetric.

\looseness=-1 The median carries all confirmatory statistical weight. The 99th
percentile is reported for every cell but is only indicative, at any trial count
used here. The $p_{99}$ indicative-only status rests on a measured
bootstrap-coverage gap, not a generic caveat about percentile bootstraps (see
supplementary material for the coverage simulation and exact figures). A cell's
$p_{99}$ rests on its top $\sim$1\% of values (10 of 1000 trials, 50 of 5000),
and a percentile bootstrap can reweight those but never exceed the largest value
already drawn. The gap is real and in the predicted direction but modest; more
trials help $p_{99}$ without closing it.
